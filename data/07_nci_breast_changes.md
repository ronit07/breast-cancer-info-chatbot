## Common benign breast changes
Many noncancerous (benign) conditions can cause lumps or lumpiness in the breast. Some cannot be felt and are found only on a mammogram or during a biopsy done for another reason. Common benign conditions include adenosis, breast cysts (usually fluid-filled), duct ectasia, hematoma, fat necrosis, fibroadenomas, fibrocystic breast changes, intraductal papilloma, lipoma and mastitis. Symptoms of benign conditions can include breast pain, nipple discharge, inverted or darker nipples, and red or dimpled skin.

## Benign conditions that slightly raise risk
Some benign conditions slightly raise breast cancer risk, including complex fibroadenomas, multiple intraductal papillomas, radial scars and sclerosing adenosis. Having one does not mean cancer will develop. Phyllodes tumors are usually benign but can sometimes be cancerous.

## Precancerous conditions
Precancerous conditions are not cancer but may become cancer. They include atypical ductal hyperplasia, atypical lobular hyperplasia, lobular carcinoma in situ and pleomorphic lobular carcinoma in situ. Doctors may recommend more frequent physical exams or imaging, and sometimes hormone therapy to lower risk.

## When to see a doctor
It is important to contact your doctor if you notice any changes in your breasts. Biopsies are used to check what a change is, and mammograms can detect some benign conditions.
