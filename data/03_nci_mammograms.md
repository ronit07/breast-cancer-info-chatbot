## What a mammogram is
A mammogram is a low-dose x-ray image of the breasts. A screening mammogram checks people without symptoms. A diagnostic mammogram is done after a lump or other change is found; it takes images from more angles, so the radiation dose is higher. A radiologist reviews the images and compares them with past mammograms when possible.

## Who should be screened and when
The U.S. Preventive Services Task Force recommends a screening mammogram every 2 years for women at average risk between ages 40 and 74. People at higher risk may be advised to start earlier or be screened more often. For example, people treated with chest radiation for childhood cancer may start at age 25 or 8 years after radiation, whichever is later. People with harmful BRCA1 or BRCA2 changes may consider earlier or more frequent screening.

## What to expect
The breast is pressed between two plates and several images are taken. Compression can be painful for some people; over-the-counter pain medicine beforehand may help. Avoid scheduling just before or during your period. On the test day do not use deodorant, antiperspirant, powder, lotion, cream or perfume on the breasts or underarms. People with implants should tell the facility. 3D mammography (digital breast tomosynthesis) finds tumors better than standard mammography alone when combined with it, but it is not yet known whether it lowers breast cancer deaths more; the TMIST trial is studying this.

## How results are reported (BI-RADS)
Results usually arrive within about 2 weeks. BI-RADS 0: more imaging needed. 1: negative, continue regular screening. 2: benign finding, continue regular screening. 3: probably benign, follow-up mammogram in 6 months. 4: suspicious abnormality, biopsy may be needed. 5: highly suggestive of cancer, biopsy required. 6: biopsy-proven cancer already known. Most people who are called back for more imaging or a biopsy do not have breast cancer.

## Dense breasts
Breast density describes the relative amounts of dense and fatty tissue on a mammogram. Mammography is more likely to miss cancer in women with dense breasts.
