## Treatment overview
Several treatments are available for breast cancer. The patient and their cancer care team choose a plan, which may combine more than one type of treatment. Approaches vary by breast cancer type and stage.

## Local and systemic treatments
Local treatments target the area with cancer: surgery (lumpectomy and mastectomy are the two main types) and radiation therapy. Systemic treatments are drugs that reach cancer cells throughout the body: chemotherapy, hormone therapy, immunotherapy and targeted therapy.

## Which systemic treatment is used
Not everyone receives chemotherapy. Hormone therapy helps lower the risk of recurrence for certain breast cancers. Immunotherapy may be used for triple-negative breast cancer. Targeted therapy is used for certain breast cancers.

## Special situations
Treatment depends strongly on stage. NCI also has sections on breast cancer during pregnancy, ductal carcinoma in situ (DCIS, abnormal cells in the milk ducts), inflammatory breast cancer, triple-negative breast cancer, and Paget disease of the breast (a rare type involving the nipple and areola).

## Clinical trials
Clinical trials are an option for people with breast cancer. NCI's clinical trial search and the Cancer Information Service can help find enrolling trials.
