## Hormone receptor and HER2 testing
Estrogen receptor (ER) and progesterone receptor (PR): a tumor is called positive if 1% or more of cells stain, measured mainly by immunohistochemistry (IHC). HER2 is measured by IHC or in situ hybridization (ISH): IHC 0 or 1+ is negative, 2+ is equivocal and needs confirmatory testing, 3+ is positive.

## Subtypes
Tumors are grouped as hormone receptor positive, HER2 positive, or triple negative (ER, PR and HER2 all negative). ER, PR and HER2 status help predict prognosis and response to endocrine (hormone) therapy and HER2-directed therapy.

## Gene expression profile (Oncotype DX)
For HER2-negative, hormone receptor-positive disease, the Oncotype DX recurrence score helps guide chemotherapy decisions. Node-negative: scores of 11 or lower do not call for chemotherapy; 12 to 25 call for an individualized decision considering age, tumor features and patient preference; above 25 indicates chemotherapy. Postmenopausal, node-positive: 25 or lower does not indicate chemotherapy; above 25 does. In the TAILORx trial, chemotherapy was unlikely to give substantial benefit to women over 50 with node-negative disease and scores of 11 to 25.
