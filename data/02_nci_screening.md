## What screening is
Screening looks for breast cancer in people without symptoms. It can find cancer at an earlier, more treatable stage. Mammography is the standard screening test for most women.

## Breast MRI
Breast MRI uses radio waves, a strong magnet and a computer to make detailed images. It does not use radiation. High-risk women may have MRI along with mammography because MRI detects cancer more sensitively and mammography alone may miss some cancers in these women. Women with dense breasts may be offered MRI, but it is not known whether adding MRI improves health outcomes for them.

## Clinical breast exam, ultrasound and self-exam
In a clinical breast exam a provider feels the breasts and underarms for lumps. It is not an adequate screening test on its own. Breast ultrasound uses sound waves and may be offered in addition to mammography for women with dense breasts, but its effect on health outcomes is unknown. Women should know how their breasts normally feel and report changes to their doctor; breast self-exam alone is not an adequate screening test.

## Benefits and harms of screening
Benefit: finding cancer before symptoms appear lets treatment start earlier. Harms include false-positive results (distress and follow-up biopsies, more common in younger women, women with dense breasts, prior biopsies, family history, or estrogen use), overdiagnosis and overtreatment (finding cancers that would never have caused problems), delayed diagnosis from false-negative results (more common with dense breasts), and a very small radiation risk from mammography.
